## 1. What actually enforces the vendor windows?

**Negative finding within pinned, inspected vendor VIDC call chain:** `LowTension/android_kernel_xiaomi_sm8475@39382bf760ccf05a8fb4dddee7138ecac02d4a59`, `techpack/video/driver/vidc/src/msm_vidc_dt.c::msm_vidc_populate_context_bank` lines 848–861 parses `virtual-addr-pool` into `cb->addr_range`. Lines 873–875 and 819–821 log that range; `msm_vidc_setup_context_bank` lines 784–815 selects `cb->dev`, obtains `iommu_get_domain_for_dev`, enables vendor `iommu_dma_enable_best_fit_algo`, sets DMA segment limits. It **does not set the IOVA allocator lower bound or domain geometry**. `msm_vidc_memory.c::get_context_bank` lines 27–62 chooses the context device by buffer role; `msm_vidc_memory_map` lines 182–280 calls `dma_buf_attach(dmabuf, cb->dev)` and `dma_buf_map_attachment` and consumes the returned SG DMA address, **without checking against `cb->addr_range`**. Allocation of internal DMA heap buffers similarly does not pass the pool start/end as an allocator argument. This directly proves the inspected VIDC parse/map path does not enforce the range, **not** that the downstream IOMMU provider, vendor DMA-IOMMU internals, firmware or silicon cannot impose a constraint. Vendor `iommu_dma_enable_best_fit_algo` is a placement preference, not evidence of minimum/maximum bounds. There is no source-grounded basis yet to call `0x25800000` a mandatory *hardware* floor. No claim of Motorola's exact running video driver.

The two Motorola source-hashed compiled FDT candidates specify nonpixel SID/mask `0x2180/0x400`, vendor pool `[0x25800000,0xe0000000)`; pixel SID/mask `0x2187/0x400`, pool `[0x00100000,0xe0000000)`. Those properties establish a vendor **declared policy**; they do not prove where it is enforced or that every firmware address must fall within those ranges. **Necessity remains unresolved**; do not either discard the floor as irrelevant or promote it to a silicon limit.

## 2. Pinned mainline enforcement options and timing

- `Drzet/linux:razr/vpu@869cf4a6ad21afcc2435c4e2d1d4fa9ab33fb4bf drivers/iommu/dma-iommu.c::iommu_dma_init_domain` lines 670–723 initializes `iovad->start_pfn = max(1, domain->geometry.aperture_start >> order)` **once**, when `iommu_setup_dma_ops` lines 2145–2162 configures DMA operations on device/domain. A subsequent differing start returns `-EFAULT`. `iommu_dma_alloc_iova` lines 760–803 allocates from that domain's IOVA allocator and applies DMA mask/bus limit/forced upper geometry. It is used for normal DMA SG mapping and coherent allocations (call sites lines 866, 974, 1507, 1827, 2225). **Thus a correctly initialized domain-wide IOVA start would cover both imported DMA-BUF attachments and HFI/internal `dma_alloc_attrs`** on that child device. No separate VB2 allocator is needed.
- `iova_reserve_iommu_regions` lines 553–589 calls `iommu_get_resv_regions` during domain initialization. It reserves specified windows, but the pinned ARM SMMU provider's ordinary reserved-region semantics are **not** a reviewed oneli-specific arbitrary lower-window API. Inventing a gigantic reserved range or retroactively editing `iovad` would be a core policy hack.
- `drivers/of/device.c::of_dma_configure_id` lines 83–168 reads `dma-ranges`, establishes device DMA range map and masks, calls `of_iommu_configure` then `arch_setup_dma_ops`. `iommu_dma_init_domain` consults the map for **compatibility** (lines 688–694) but initializes its lower bound from **domain geometry**, not `dma_range_map_min`. Therefore a child `dma-ranges` entry is **not a supported replacement for the vendor IOVA floor**. DMA masks cap the top, never the bottom.
- **Smallest genuine core change if strict vendor floors become a reviewed requirement:** a scoped, documented **pre-domain-initialization IOVA aperture policy** supported by IOMMU/DMA core and applied to the *child's domain* before `iommu_setup_dma_ops`. Its exact public API, ownership, DT binding and integration point do **not exist as established interfaces in the pinned sources**. Such a change would need a separate reviewed IOMMU-core design; do not call it an existing function, write directly to `domain->geometry` after setup, mutate private DMA cookie or manually map outside VB2. A post-map `[start,end)` check and immediate unwind can **detect** an out-of-window IOVA and fail closed, but cannot force the allocator to choose a legal one. This may be an initial experimental diagnostic guard only, **not enforcement equivalence**.
- **If hardware floor is not required:** ordinary two-child DMA-API domains with source-matched SIDs and conservative upper masks may be enough for an isolated disabled *diagnostic* prototype; do not claim vendor pool fidelity or activate the driver without evidence. No native firmware ACK is required before developing that disabled prototype.

## 3. Concrete proposed topology and lifecycle patch sketch (not committed kernel code)

**Binding candidate (review required, not upstream-approved):** Extend `Documentation/devicetree/bindings/media/qcom,sm8475-iris.yaml` to allow two role-named **DMA consumer child platform nodes** under the disabled oneli video parent. Each has one `iommus` reference: nonpixel `<&apps_smmu 0x2180 0x400>`, pixel `<&apps_smmu 0x2187 0x400>`; require explicit `compatible` or reviewed match/population mechanism, `#address-cells` only if actually needed, `status = "disabled"` inherited from parent, and prohibit secure contexts. The exact child compatible is **unselected** until binding review. A parent with both SIDs does not give separate domains; avoid parent data DMA ownership.

**Probe / binding sketch:**
1. Register a small child platform driver (or justified OF-population+device-reference route) for **both** DMA consumers, each with its own OF `iommus` and `struct device`. Their probe stores the child role/device and obtains the *already default-attached* DMA IOMMU domain; it must not manually swap `iommu_attach_device` during streaming. OF/platform creation configures child DMA and IOMMU before child probe. Check `dev_is_dma_coherent`, DMA mask/upper limit, actual `iommu_get_dma_domain`, distinct `iommu_group_get` identities; fail closed if IOMMU was bypassed or group/domain collapsed. `of_dma_configure_id` treats non-deferral IOMMU errors as possible DMA fallback, so explicit post-probe check is necessary.
2. **Avoid parent-child circular dependency:** parent `probe` must initialize minimal core state and populate child devices **without waiting synchronously for their driver probes**; child probe may read `dev_get_drvdata(parent)` only after parent sets it, and must not require the parent to finish video registration. Parent can register V4L2/media nodes only after both child owners are ready, via a separate completion/work path or explicit reprobe/defer state machine. Merely returning `-EPROBE_DEFER` from the parent after devm child population may tear down those children on every retry and cause an endless dependency cycle. Define lifetime and locking for this state transition before implementation.
3. Hold `get_device`/matching `put_device` on each DMA owner as long as core queues or any session buffers remain; a device ref does **not** itself prevent unbind or IOMMU-domain teardown. Block removal/stop new sessions, drain VB2 and delayed DPB refs, internal buffers, HFI queue table and SFR, then release child references and depopulate. Handle partial second-child failure, deferred supplier and fault path with reverse-order unwind.

**Buffer-owner wiring sketch:** `iris_vidc.c::iris_m2m_queue_init`: nonsecure decoder OUTPUT -> nonpixel child, CAPTURE -> pixel child; encoder OUTPUT -> pixel, CAPTURE -> nonpixel. Existing `vb2_dma_contig_memops` automatically maps imported DMABUF to queue's device; reject short contiguous DMA span. `iris_buffer.c::iris_create_internal_buffer` and destroy: pixel DPB; COMV/LINE/PERSIST/BIN and supported scratch nonpixel, retaining exact `struct device *` owner for matching free; classify all actually allocated types, not just a sample. `iris_hfi_queue.c::iris_iface_queues_init/deinit`: queue table, command/message/debug and SFR on nonpixel child. Secure sessions unsupported and rejected. All allocations, imported maps, and free/unmap must stay on same device; never copy one IOVA to the other domain.

**Negative tests before active integration:** two different DMA IOMMU groups/domains; no DMA fallback; absence/duplicate child, deferred SMMU, missing/invalid aperture; no parent/child probe deadlock; correct coherent and DMABUF mapping ownership; same DMABUF independently attached in two domains; complete SG span/overflow and out-of-range rejection; HFI and DPB lifetime through streamoff/fault/unbind; correct reverse unwind. Host compilation/schema validates code only; device IOVA and HFI outcomes deferred.

## Stop/next smallest decision

**Result:** downstream VIDC pool is parsed and logged but not enforced in its traced attach/map path; actual downstream IOMMU allocator/provider enforcement and the *necessity* of each minimum are **not established**. Pinned upstream has a domain-geometry lower-bound allocator, but no reviewed oneli per-child pre-initialization policy/binding to feed it; masks and `dma-ranges` do not solve that. Therefore **no source-justified strict-window implementation** and **no kernel patch** in this round. Coordinator must explicitly choose (a) review a tiny IOMMU-core pre-init aperture interface only if strict floors become justified, or (b) permit a *disabled diagnostic two-child ownership prototype* with ordinary domain allocations, no claimed vendor-window equivalence, and runtime window logging/guard. Boot owns any shared DTS application. This is an offline engineering hold, **not a request for phone access or native firmware ACK**.
